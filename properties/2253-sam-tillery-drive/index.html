Why I like this one

The entire home feels bright and spacious. It has the openness people want in a newer rental without trying to squeeze too much into the floor plan, and the single-story layout is straightforward and easy to live in.

The lot is an even bigger differentiator. This is the largest lot in the neighborhood, with a very big front lawn and no home directly behind it. That gives the property noticeably more breathing room than the typical new-construction lot and should make it more appealing both to tenants and, eventually, to buyers.

I also like that the investment case does not need an aggressive appreciation story to make sense. The reason I would buy this one is the combination of good cash flow, a practical commuter location, the Bryant school zone, a strong family-rental layout, and a lot that stands out inside the community.

Several rent-ready items are included by the builder.

The builder will include a refrigerator, washer, dryer and a fence. Those are expenses an investor would otherwise normally face immediately after closing, so having them included reduces setup work and makes the home more turnkey for the first tenant.

The submarket

Alexander is a small community on the southwest side of the Little Rock metropolitan area. The appeal here is that a tenant can live in a newer suburban neighborhood with more house and yard for the money while remaining within an easy commute of the region’s main employment, medical, government and business center in Little Rock.

Midland Estates sits about four minutes from I-30, the main interstate connecting this side of the metro with Little Rock. From the neighborhood, downtown Little Rock is roughly a 20-minute drive in normal traffic. Bryant and Benton are the larger nearby suburban communities along the same I-30 corridor, so residents also have convenient access to the retail, restaurants, services and everyday shopping concentrated there.

The home is in the Bryant School District, which is an important part of the location story for family renters. The combination of the school zone, direct interstate access and a newer suburban setting is why this area competes well with other southwest Little Rock suburbs for households that do not need to live in the city itself.
